City guide

New to Pune? Here is the honest version.

Written for people moving here for work — not tourists. What the city runs on, what it costs, how the commute works, and what to expect month to month.

Why people come

Pune runs on three engines: the MIDC factory belt (Bhosari, Chakan, Talegaon), IT parks (Hinjawadi), and education. The Pimpri-Chinchwad side — where the PG sits — is the factory belt: steady shift work, lower living costs than the city core, and everything a working person needs within a short ride.

Getting around

Two-wheelers rule this city — cheap to run, fastest door to gate. PMPML buses cover the whole PCMC side, the metro is expanding on the Pimpri-Chinchwad corridor, and autos and ride-hailing fill the gaps. Live near your workplace and you win the commute game entirely.

Weather, month by month

March to May runs hot — plan indoor time in the afternoons. June to September is monsoon: intense but dramatic, and the hills around the city turn green. October to February is the reward: dry, pleasant, sometimes needing a light jacket at night.

Cost of living

The PCMC and MIDC belt is the budget-friendly side of Pune: food, transport and rent all run below the city core. A PG inside the industrial belt keeps your two biggest costs — rent and commute — small at the same time. That is more salary that actually reaches your savings.

Food

Maharashtrian staples everywhere: misal, vada pav, poha, thali meals that fill you for little. The industrial belt is packed with mess-style eateries built for shift workers. Home-style cooking — the kind a PG kitchen makes — beats eating out every day, for health and for your wallet.

Culture

Pune wears its traditions openly — Ganesh Chaturthi here is a festival the whole city participates in, and Diwali lights up every lane. Temples, forts and hill treks sit within weekend distance. The pace is friendlier than Mumbai: small-town warmth, big-city opportunity.
